# research:lit-agentic-rl


## question

What training scale and recipe make RL of small open-weight models on multi-turn tool/terminal/coding tasks produce solid, replicated capability gains, and what does that imply for a 4B LoRA model trained from an SFT checkpoint (plan 20: plain-U GRPO from G2, 150 steps x 16 prompts x 8 generations = 19,200 episodes over 800 rows, lr 2e-5, KL beta 0.04)?

## headline

Every published positive result for RL of a small (2B-32B) model on multi-turn terminal/SWE/tool tasks that states its scale used 24,600 to 128,000 on-policy episodes, 240-512 episodes per update, and KL beta of 0 to 0.001; the gains work out to roughly 0.07-0.25 held-out points per 1,000 episodes (my cross-paper arithmetic, 7 recipes). Plan 20's 19,200 episodes per arm at 128 per update is below the smallest of them (78% of OpenThoughts-Agent's 24,576; 15% of TMax's 128,000), so at published rates it buys about +1 to +5 points of success, which is the plan's own 'hope' and sits at its MDE (2.5-3.6 U); all 8 past RL runs in this project total 10,880 episodes and the longest (3,200) is 13% of the smallest published positive budget. The closest published analogue is TMax, which took the same base model, Qwen3.5-4B, from 33.6 to 54.3 on Terminal-Bench Lite and 15.5 to 24.6 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 with 500 steps x (8 prompts x 32 generations), lr 1e-6, no KL, zero-variance filtering with active sampling and an FP32 LM head; it also got +12.6 / +7.9 on the 9B model using Endless Terminals tasks as the only RL data. Unique tasks are not the binding constraint (72 to ~800 tasks gave most of the gain in LOOP, River, TMax-3.1k, SETA); total episodes, signal density and collapse control are. Two things do not transfer: G2 already passes 79% of train-pool episodes (60.5% of variants pass 4/4), and its failures are confident wrong claims (56% of failing episodes) rather than loops or turn exhaustion (1.5% and 4.2%), so the realistic route to a large U gain is the ternary reward converting false claims, which TruthRL shows can move accuracy-minus-hallucination by +16 points over success-only RL at 7-8B, at a cost of 4-8 accuracy points.


## results


**1. RECIPE - TMax (same base model family as this project, incl. Qwen3.5-4B). DPPO = GRPO with a mask on tokens whose sampler and trainer probabilities differ (binary total-variation threshold 0.1); token-level loss; centred advantages (no std scaling); KL beta 0; zero-std groups filtered and batches refilled by active sampling; no SFT for Qwen3.5/3.6 (existing SFT data cost Qwen3.5 up to 10 points); FP32 LM head 'especially crucial when training Qwen 3.5'. LoRA vs full fine-tuning is not stated.**

- *numbers*: 8 prompts x 32 generations = 256 episodes/step; 500 steps = 128,000 kept episodes (derived; more were generated because ~40% of groups are all-zero); lr 1e-6 constant AdamW, grad-norm 1.0; temperature 1.0; 64 tool calls; 65,536 tokens per episode; 13,565 tasks (<= 4,000 prompt slots, so under one pass). Results, mean +- SE over 3 trials: Qwen3.5-4B TB-Lite 33.6+-1.8 -> 54.3+-1.9, TB 2.1 15.5+-1.5 -> 24.6+-1.8; 2B 7.1 -> 18.2 and 1.9 -> 5.3; 9B 49.4 -> 65.6 and 20.5 -> 29.6; 27B 74.2 -> 77.4 and 43.6 -> 43.9 (almost nothing when the start is strong); Qwen3-8B base 5.0 -> SFT 9.1 -> RL 17.8 (TB-Lite), 1.1 -> 5.3 -> 6.4 (TB 2.1). 9B trained on Endless Terminals tasks only: 62.0 / 28.4 vs 49.4 / 20.5 without RL. 9B on a 3.1k-task subset: 62.0 / 27.3 (about 75-78% of the full-data gain, derived). Runs 'often collapsing past 300 steps' before the FP32 head, larger groups and less infrastructure noise. Checkpoint chosen by best TB-Lite among evaluations every 100 steps.
- *source*: TMax: A simple recipe for terminal agents, arXiv 2606.23321 (June 2026), Tables 2, 3, 13 and appendix E; fetched three times (body twice, appendix once)
- *confidence*: verified

**2. RECIPE - Endless Terminals (the environment source used here). Vanilla PPO in SkyRL, binary end-of-episode reward, DAPO-style clip 0.2/0.28, sequence-mean loss, no KL ('anecdotally we found that this hurt performance'). Learning rate, prompts per step, number of steps and total episodes are NOT reported in v2 or v3.**

- *numbers*: 16 rollouts per prompt; <= 16 turns in training (64 at evaluation); 16k-token context; 2,048 tokens per turn; temperature 0.6; 3,255 tasks; 4 x A100 for ~2 days (3B, 7B), 8 x B200 for ~8 h (8B SFT). Held-out dev: Llama-3.2-3B 4.0 -> 18.2; Qwen2.5-7B 10.7 -> 53.3; Qwen3-8B-openthinker-sft 42.6 -> 59.0 (+16.4 from an SFT start). Terminal-Bench 2.0 (5 runs, no SE): 0.0 -> 2.2; 2.2 -> 3.4; 1.1 -> 6.7. Failure analysis of the best model on TB2: loops 39%, turn exhaustion 26%, early wrong answer 49%. About half the tasks are solved 16/16 by the filter model.
- *source*: Endless Terminals: Scaling RL Environments for Terminal Agents, arXiv 2601.16443 v2 and v3 (Jan-Feb 2026); both versions fetched
- *confidence*: verified

**3. RECIPE - River (RL from an SFT checkpoint at 8B, 3 seeds). GRPO with std-normalised advantages, clip 0.2/0.28, sequence-mean loss, no KL, binary reward plus a repetition penalty (0.05 per repeated turn, cap 0.15); environments filtered for verifier quality (more than 60% of TMax environments had at least one quality issue; oracle pass@2 = 0 removed). Rewarding 'verify before done' gave no gain. With partial rewards on unfiltered environments the agent 'prematurely claims task completion' and generalisation fell while in-distribution reward rose.**

- *numbers*: 32 prompts x 8 = 256 episodes/step, 2 update epochs per batch, 400 steps = 102,400 episodes (derived); lr 5e-7; <= 16 turns; 16,384 input tokens; temperature 0.6; 3.5K environments; ~60 h on 8 x H200. Qwen3-8B, mean +- std over 3 seeds, average of 4 terminal benchmarks: SFT 12.5+-1.1 -> SFT+RL 19.4+-1.2 (+6.9); TB 2.1 6.4 -> 9.7; RL on a random 3.5K subset 17.7+-0.7. Breadth test (held-out solve rate): SFT 32.3; RL on 8 domains 53.9; 4 domains 53.6; 2 domains (~800 environments) 49.2, i.e. 78% of the gain (derived).
- *source*: Learning Generalizable Behaviors for Terminal Agents, arXiv 2608.22631 v3 (Aug-Sep 2026), Tables 2, 4, 8
- *confidence*: verified

**4. RECIPE - OpenThoughts-Agent (RL from a 'cold' SFT 8B checkpoint). RLOO with per-prompt std normalisation, clip 0.2, no KL, no entropy bonus. The run peaked and then collapsed; the released checkpoint is from before the collapse. 'Undertrained' SFT models benefit more from RL; base Qwen3-8B in that harness could not benefit from RL at all.**

- *numbers*: 64 prompts x 8 = 512 episodes/step, 48 steps, 2 epochs = 24,576 episodes (derived); lr 5e-6 constant; 32,768-token context; turns unbounded (1,800 s timeout); 24 x A100 for ~46 h. RL-specific gain about +5 points on the core benchmarks over SFT-only (+5.4 on SWE-bench Verified); run-to-run spread 1.6-2.0 points. Training reward peaked near 0.51 then fell to ~0.13 with ~80% agent timeouts; deployed checkpoint is step 45. RL raised tool calls +31% and think tokens +116%.
- *source*: OpenThoughts-Agent: Data Recipes for Agentic Models, arXiv 2606.24855 (June 2026), section 5 and appendices D-F
- *confidence*: verified

## design_recommendations


**1. 1 (well-supported). Raise the per-arm on-policy budget from 19,200 to at least 51,200 episodes and the batch from 128 to 256 episodes per update: 200 steps x (16 prompts x 16 generations), or 400 steps x 128 if memory forces it. Pay for it by cutting arms: A1 + A2 (seeds) + B at 51,200 each (153,600 episodes, 256-483 GPU-hours) instead of five arms at 19,200 (96,000 episodes, 160-300 GPU-hours). Drop arm C and the third seed first. Reach the episode count with more passes over the in-band rows (8+ visits per row instead of 3), not by hunting for more unique tasks.**

- *rationale*: Every stated positive budget is 24,576-128,000 episodes at 240-512 per update (OpenThoughts-Agent, SkyRL-Agent, River, DeepSWE, LOOP, TMax). TMax did this on Qwen3.5-4B itself (+20.7 TB-Lite, +9.1 TB 2.1 at 128,000). The 8 past runs here stopped at 448-3,200 episodes. Breadth is not the constraint: LOOP gained +27 to +32 points from 72 tasks at ~1,770 episodes per task; River kept 78% of its gain with ~800 environments; the S=8 band supplies only about 830 [730, 1,006] in-band feasible/control rows anyway.
- *expected_measurable_effect*: At the published 0.07-0.25 points per 1,000 episodes: held-out task success on feasible + control strata +3.6 to +12.8 points at 51,200 episodes versus +1.3 to +4.8 at 19,200 (SFT-start <= 8B rates: +1.5 to +10 versus +0.6 to +3.9), capped by G2's best-of-4 ceiling (about +8 to +13). That moves the expected capability part of delta-U from at-or-below the MDE (2.5-3.6) to about 1-3x the MDE. Measure: paired delta task success and delta-U on held-out at S=16, plus select-split U every 25 steps to show the learning curve.
- *confidence*: medium

**2. 2 (well-supported). Use group size 16 and add online zero-variance filtering with stratified active sampling: after rollouts, drop groups whose 16 rewards are all equal and refill from the same stratum (plain / lone control / each twin kind) until the step has its 16 informative groups, keeping the 12 feasible : 4 twin composition. Draw from the whole train-legal pool with a wider prescreen band instead of a fixed 800-row file; keep always-pass rows only as refill candidates (they enter when the policy starts failing them, which is the retention guard the plan wants).**

- *rationale*: DAPO's dynamic sampling was its largest single step (+8 AIME points); TMax filters zero-std groups and refills, and found larger groups alleviated collapse (32 vs 8); POLARIS drops fully solved items; Online Difficulty Filtering reports +10 AIME (search summary). Locally, 24.4% of in-band G=8 groups will have no pass/fail contrast at training time, 12.8% at G=16, 6.2% at G=32, and the plan deliberately adds 75 always-pass rows and 16 revoke pairs with zero gradient.
- *expected_measurable_effect*: `zero_variance_group_share` in the loss goes from a predicted ~24% (plus ~11% retention rows) to 0 by construction; gradient-carrying episodes per step rise from roughly 65-75% to 100% of the batch at an oversampling cost of about 1.15x at G=16. Log the oversampling factor and the per-stratum refill counts; expect the usable pool to grow from ~43% to ~52% of feasible/control variants at G=16 (60% at G=32).
- *confidence*: high

**3. 3 (well-supported). Replace 'candidate = step 150, no selection' with a pre-registered selection rule on train-side data: evaluate the select split (U at S=4) every 25 steps, candidate = the checkpoint with the best 3-point-smoothed select U among checkpoints where every guard passes; evaluate held-out once on that checkpoint. Keep checkpoints every 5 steps.**

- *rationale*: For runs longer than ~50 updates the documented failure is peak-then-collapse, not overfitting: OpenThoughts-Agent's reward fell from 0.51 to 0.13 after step 45 and the released model is the pre-collapse checkpoint; TMax collapsed past 300 steps before its fixes and selects by best dev score every 100 steps; GRPO tool-use runs on Qwen2.5-3B/7B collapse within 300 steps; LOOP reports its best checkpoint (iteration 530). A fixed end step throws away the peak if recommendation 1 is adopted.
- *expected_measurable_effect*: If a collapse occurs after the peak, the candidate keeps the peak (in OpenThoughts' case the difference between +5 and a broken model). Cost: selection bias on select only, bounded by the select-split SE; report select U and held-out U side by side so the optimism is visible. Published gains are themselves dev-selected, so this also makes the comparison fair.
- *confidence*: high

**4. 4 (capability effect well-supported; honesty retention at low beta is speculative, so make it the single-factor arm). Run the KL coefficient as the third arm in place of prompt diversity: A at beta 0.04 as planned, A-lowKL at beta 0.001 (the largest value any positive agent recipe states), same seed and data. Protect the SFT-taught honesty with what the literature shows works, reward-side: keep the twin prompts at 25% of the batch (never below 10%), the claim-given-pass stop rule and the twin read/claim probes.**

- *rationale*: Beta 0.04 is 40x the largest stated value. Endless Terminals: KL 'hurt performance'; LOOP: -1 to -3 points with KL; TMax, River, OpenThoughts, SkyRL-Agent, DeepSWE, DAPO, POLARIS use 0. Hallucination Tax shows refusal is preserved by mixing 10% decline-is-correct items into RL (0.01 -> 0.73-0.99 at 7-8B) with beta only 0.001; plan 20 already has 25% twins. Locally beta 0.04 did not stop a -3.4 [-6.9, +0.1] success change in 30 steps, and a KL on trained tokens let actions drift in iteration 17, so the KL is neither sufficient nor clearly necessary.
- *expected_measurable_effect*: A-lowKL minus A on held-out task success: +1 to +3 points if LOOP's ablation transfers; KL to G2 at step 200 several times higher than in A (log it; do not stop on it in this arm). Honesty cost is read on twin claim rate, P(claim | pass) and the read-before-destruction guard; if those guards fail in A-lowKL and pass in A, the KL is doing real work and the result is still informative.
- *confidence*: medium

**5. 5 (well-supported for Qwen3.5 by TMax; local harm unmeasured). Close the sampler-trainer numerical gap before the long run: add a DPPO-style token mask (exclude from the loss any token whose sampler and trainer probabilities differ by more than 0.1 in binary total variation, using the vLLM logprobs already collected), and test whether the LM head can be computed in fp32 for this tied-embedding model (or sampled in fp32). Decide with a 20-step pilot.**

- *rationale*: TMax: FP32 LM head 'especially crucial when training Qwen 3.5, but not when training Qwen 3', and this was one of three fixes separating collapse at ~300 steps from smooth training to 1,000. Here `cast_lm_head_to_fp32` is false and 4.1% of loss tokens (3.6-5.0% per step) have importance ratios more than 5% from 1 from step 1, with minima down to 0.005-0.05; the correction is a truncated ratio, not a mask. The risk grows with run length, which recommendation 1 increases.
- *expected_measurable_effect*: `sampling/importance_sampling_ratio/outside_band_share` from ~4% to under 1% with an fp32 head, or masked share ~4% with the DPPO mask and min ratio above ~0.9; no change expected in the first 50 steps, the benefit is stability at 150-400 steps (no late reward collapse). If the 20-step pilot shows identical train reward and KL with and without the mask, keep the cheaper path.
- *confidence*: medium

**6. 6 (well-supported). Keep the ternary U reward at c = 1 with no extra shaping terms, keep arm B (success-only) as the control, and state the expected mechanism in the pre-registration: most of any U gain will come from false claims turning into declines, with a success cost that must stay inside the guard. Add one report line: delta-U decomposed into the false-claim term and the pass term, for A and B.**

- *rationale*: TruthRL's ternary reward (same +1 / 0 / -1) beat success-only RL on accuracy-minus-hallucination by +16.4 at 8B (37.2 vs 20.8) by halving hallucination (39.5 -> 19.4) at -3.7 accuracy; its more elaborate reward variants did not beat the plain ternary; success-only RL drove abstention to 0-1% (and Hallucination Tax: refusal 0.30 -> 0.08). River: rewarding verification added nothing. The over-abstention risk is real (TruthRL's weak-verifier run and the Qwen3-4B risk-threshold run both over-rejected; the it19c method arm collapsed to non-claims in 7 steps), so the P(claim | pass) stop rule is the right guard. Locally 56.2% [48.3, 64.5] of G2's failing episodes are false claims, each worth +1 U as a decline and +2 U as a pass.
- *expected_measurable_effect*: A minus B on held-out: false claims per 100 episodes lower in A by several points and task success lower by 0-4 points (TruthRL direction); B expected to raise claims on twins. If A's false-claim term improves by even a third of the 13.8 U false-claim headroom that is +4.6 U, above the MDE, independent of capability. Measure with the existing U estimator plus the two-term decomposition.
- *confidence*: medium

**7. 7 (speculative). Add one context arm D: the same U reward and recipe started from base Qwen3.5-4B instead of G2, at the same 51,200 episodes, reported against base (and against G2 as context), not as the H1 candidate.**

- *rationale*: The strongest published gains for this exact model come from RL on the base with no SFT (TMax-4B +20.7 / +9.1; SFT data cost Qwen3.5 up to 10 points), and gains shrink as the SFT start gets stronger (DeepSWE none; WebAgent-R1 +5.8 vs +13.9; OpenThoughts 'undertrained SFT models benefit more'; TMax-27B +0.3). Base has ~30 U of headroom on the internal held-out set (U 2.7 vs G2 33.3, claims on impossible tasks 52% vs 13.7%, from u_checks.txt) and far more reward variance on twins. TruthRL moved the same metric by +32 to +34 points from untrained 7-8B models with a ternary reward, single-turn. The past base-start runs here ended at 1,152-3,200 episodes, 1-2.5% of TMax's budget.
- *expected_measurable_effect*: Base -> D on held-out U: anything from +5 to +30 U would be a measurable, RL-only result on the core claim with an MDE far below the effect; the comparison D vs G2 says how much of G2's honesty RL alone can reach. Risk: D may not approach G2 (33.3) and costs one GPU for 3.6-6.7 days.
- *confidence*: low

**8. 8 (speculative; a measurement before any change). Before freezing temperature 1.0, run G2 on ~50 in-band prompts x 8 at temperature 1.0, 1.2 and 1.4 and record pass@1, tool-call parse failures, share of groups with mixed rewards, and distinct command sequences per group. Adopt a higher training temperature only if the mixed-group share rises by at least 10 points with under 2 points of pass@1 loss; evaluate at the usual setting either way.**

- *rationale*: G2's token entropy (0.153) is half the base model's (0.26-0.30) and below DeepSWE's 0.3-1 range; RC-GRPO reports SFT-then-GRPO stalling for lack of within-group variance in multi-turn tool calling; POLARIS found 1.0 too low for Qwen3-4B on maths and trains at 1.4. Against: Endless Terminals and River train at 0.6, and one workshop study found extra rollout entropy did not help. No agent temperature sweep exists, so this has to be measured here.
- *expected_measurable_effect*: Mixed-reward group share at G=8 on in-band prompts (predicted ~76% at temperature 1.0) and the zero-variance oversampling factor; decision recorded in the pre-registration. About 1,200 episodes (2-4 GPU-hours).
- *confidence*: low

**9. 9 (well-supported; mostly 'do not change'). Keep: lr 2e-5 with r 32 / alpha 64 on attention + MLP; `scale_rewards: none`; no entropy bonus; 36 turns and 16,384 tokens per episode; no repetition penalty; binary hidden-test outcome rather than partial test credit. Add Terminal-Bench Lite (script already in the repo) as a secondary external endpoint beside TB2.**

- *rationale*: lr: published LoRA RL spans 1e-6 to 5e-5 (LOOP 5e-5 at r 16; PeRL 1e-5 = 5e-6; 10x rule from full-FT 1e-6 to 5e-6 gives 1e-5 to 5e-5), and the repo's bf16 merge floor argues against going lower. Reward scaling: LOOP -9 points with normalisation. Entropy bonus: DeepSWE instability. Budget: only 4.2% [1.5, 7.6] of G2's failures hit a budget and truncated share in training is 0.1-1.0%; Endless Terminals and River train at 16 turns / 16k. Repetition: loop-dominated episodes are 1.5% [0.0, 3.5] of G2's failures. Partial credit: River saw premature completion claims and worse generalisation with partial rewards on imperfect verifiers, which is the opposite of what a claim-scored reward needs. TB-Lite: TMax publishes base Qwen3.5-4B at 33.6+-1.8 and the RL'd 4B at 54.3+-1.9 (3 trials), a far more sensitive capability endpoint than TB2 (plan MDE about 9).
- *expected_measurable_effect*: No effect expected from the keeps (they avoid known regressions of 2-9 points). TB-Lite: SE about 1.8 per arm at 3 trials on 100 tasks, so a +5 point capability change is detectable; harness differences mean only the within-harness delta is comparable to TMax (their 9B scored 29.0-49.4 depending on harness).
- *confidence*: medium

## caveats

- Every web number was extracted by a small summarising model from the fetched page, not read by me in the PDF. I re-fetched the load-bearing ones (TMax Qwen3.5-4B rows twice, Endless Terminals v2 and v3, DeepSWE twice) and they agreed, but table and figure values could still be mis-extracted. Numbers that exist only in figures (TMax group-size ablation, River per-model Qwen3.5-4B results, DeepSWE compact-filtering ablation, Hallucination Tax pre-RL refusal rates) were not retrieved.
- Could not verify: Endless Terminals' learning rate, prompts per step, step count and total episodes (not in v2 or v3; repository config not opened). TMax: LoRA versus full fine-tuning is not stated; the claim in secondary coverage that DPPO adds 'over five Terminal-Bench points' over GRPO and that the dataset has 14,600 environments is NOT in the paper text I read (it says 13,565 tasks and only that DPPO was more stable in pilots). SETA's hyperparameter appendix, SkyRL-v0's hyperparameters, DeepSWE's learning rate and SWE-RL's learning rate and KL value were not retrievable.
- Secondary-source only (treat as not verified): ART-E hyperparameters and its accuracy (sources disagree: 96% vs ~85%); AgentGym-RL's horizon-curriculum numbers; Dr. GRPO, Online Difficulty Filtering, RL's Razor, Retaining by Doing, Tina, RC-GRPO and 'Structure Over Scale' details (search summaries or abstracts); 'When the Loop Closes' is a single-author workshop abstract.
- The 'points per 1,000 episodes' rate is my own cross-paper arithmetic. It mixes model sizes (4B-32B), benchmarks, harnesses and dev-selected checkpoints, assumes all listed steps were used, ignores episodes generated and discarded by filtering, and treats a saturating curve as linear. Use it for order of magnitude only. Most of those gains start from 5-50% success on harder benchmarks; G2 starts at 79% on its own task distribution (TMax's strong-start 27B gained +0.3 to +3.2), so the low end of the range is the more likely outcome for in-distribution success.
- No published result was found for LoRA RL of a ~4B model on a multi-turn terminal task, nor for a ternary claim-scored reward in a multi-turn agent. TruthRL and Hallucination Tax are single-turn QA with full fine-tuning; their effect sizes are evidence of direction, not of magnitude, for this project.
- Local estimates of group signal come from a beta-binomial fitted to S=4 pass counts on 400 variants (177 sources) and assume that bank is representative of the 2,699 train-legal feasible/control variants and that pass probability is fixed per variant; they cover pass/fail contrast only (under U, failing groups can still differ by claim), and they describe G2 before any policy drift. The executor's S=8 prescreen will give the real numbers; these are predictions to check against.
- The token-entropy comparison with DeepSWE's 0.3-1 range is loose: their figure is for a 32B thinking model, ours averages over tool-call JSON as well as text. The sampler-trainer drift numbers are real log values, but I did not establish that the bf16 head is their cause or that the drift harmed any run.
- Throughput (318-600 episodes per GPU-hour) is from past runs at 64 episodes per step and ~4k tokens per episode on rented cards; the plan's cluster, 128-256 episodes per step and group size 16 will change it. Group size 16 at one micro-batch per device raises trainer time per step; memory at 16 generations per prompt was not checked.
- Held-out hygiene: no path containing it19a, heldout, final or harbor was opened; directory listings showed such names but nothing in them was read. Held-out aggregates cited (G2 U 33.3, base U 2.7, claim rates) are from docs/iteration-20-research/u_checks.txt, a listed document.
